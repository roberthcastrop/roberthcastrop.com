Luxury Is Property-Specific

A luxury property cannot be valued by square footage alone

At higher price points, small differences can create substantial changes in value. Waterfront exposure, lot size, architecture, renovation quality, privacy, building reputation, views and location all influence what a buyer may be willing to pay.

The most useful analysis therefore comes from identifying the properties that truly compete with the home—not simply applying a neighborhood average.

Miami Luxury Property

Different segments require different analysis

Waterfront Homes

Water frontage, dockage, bridge access, seawall condition, lot dimensions and orientation can materially influence value.

Luxury Condos

Building reputation, floor, unit line, views, amenities, HOA expenses and assessments can create large differences between apparently similar residences.

Estate Homes

Land, privacy, architecture, renovation quality, outdoor living and the surrounding street or community become increasingly important at higher price points.

Buying Luxury Real Estate

Evaluate what cannot be seen in the listing photos

Professional photography can make several properties appear comparable online. The underlying real estate may be very different.

For luxury buyers, I look closely at comparable sales, location, property condition, ownership costs, future resale considerations and the characteristics that make the property difficult—or easy—to replace.

Selling Luxury Property

Position the property before you expose it to the market

Luxury buyers generally have alternatives. A property needs a clear reason to command attention within its price category.

That begins with pricing and presentation, but also requires identifying the property's strongest characteristics and communicating them to the buyers most likely to value them.

For a seller, the objective is not simply maximum exposure. It is the right exposure combined with a pricing and negotiation strategy designed to protect the property's value.
