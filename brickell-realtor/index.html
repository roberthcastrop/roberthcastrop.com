Buying in Brickell

Two condos in the same block can be very different investments

Brickell is one of Miami's most vertical real estate markets. Buyers are often comparing units in multiple towers where the purchase price is only one part of the decision.

Floor level, direction of the view, balcony exposure, parking, building age, reserves, monthly association fees, amenities and rental restrictions can all materially affect how one property compares with another.

That is why I look beyond price per square foot. A lower-priced unit may carry higher monthly expenses or weaker resale characteristics, while a more expensive unit may justify the difference because of its line, view, floor or building profile.

What Matters

Questions worth asking before buying a Brickell condo

The Building

How old is it? What are the association fees? Are there reserves, pending assessments or major projects? How does the building compare with newer and competing towers nearby?

The Unit

Floor, line, orientation, view, balcony, parking, condition and layout can create meaningful differences in value even inside the same building.

Your Exit Strategy

Whether you plan to live in the property, rent it or sell later, rental rules, carrying costs and buyer demand should be considered before making an offer.

Selling in Brickell

Your competition may be inside your own building

When selling a Brickell condo, the most relevant comparable is not always another unit several blocks away. Buyers frequently compare multiple units within the same building and then compare that building against nearby alternatives.

A strong pricing strategy should account for active inventory, recent sales, floor and line differences, views, upgrades, monthly costs and how your building is currently positioned against competing towers.

The goal is not simply to list the unit. It is to understand what a buyer will see when they compare your property with everything else available that week.

Living in Brickell

One of Miami's most connected urban neighborhoods

Brickell combines residential towers, offices, restaurants, shopping and waterfront access within a compact urban area. Brickell City Centre, Mary Brickell Village and the Financial District create different pockets within the neighborhood.

Access to Metromover and Metrorail, along with the ability to walk to restaurants, offices and services, is part of the appeal for residents who want an urban Miami lifestyle without depending on a car for every trip.

For buyers, location within Brickell matters. A building closer to the waterfront, transit, the Financial District or the neighborhood's retail core may appeal to a different buyer than a property only a few blocks away.

Newer vs. Established Buildings

New construction is not automatically the better choice

Brickell continues to evolve, with newer developments entering a market that already includes many established luxury towers.

New buildings can offer modern amenities, finishes and technology, while established buildings may offer larger floor plans, known operating histories and different value opportunities. The right choice depends on the buyer's priorities, carrying costs and intended use.
