A Large & Diverse Market

Kendall is not one neighborhood

Kendall covers a broad section of Miami-Dade, and the real estate market can change considerably from one community to another.

Buyers may be comparing detached homes with yards and pools against townhomes in gated communities or condos closer to major commercial corridors.

Because of that variety, understanding the immediate neighborhood and the specific property type is essential when evaluating value.

Buying in Kendall

What I would compare before making an offer

Community & Location

Access to major roads, shopping, employment areas and surrounding neighborhoods can significantly affect demand and resale value.

Property Condition

Roof age, impact windows, HVAC, plumbing, electrical systems and renovations should be considered alongside the asking price.

HOA & Restrictions

For townhomes and condos, association fees, reserves, insurance, rental restrictions and community rules can materially affect ownership costs.

Single-Family Homes

Lot, condition and upgrades can change value quickly

In Kendall, two homes with similar living area may sell at very different prices based on lot size, pool, renovation level, roof, impact protection and exact location.

Buyers looking for a house should evaluate both the property's current condition and the cost of improvements that may be needed after closing.

Townhomes & Condos

The monthly number matters

A lower purchase price does not always mean a lower total cost of ownership.

Association fees, insurance obligations, reserves, special assessments and financing requirements should all be reviewed when comparing condos and townhomes.

This is especially important when multiple communities offer similar units at apparently similar prices.

Selling in Kendall

Buyers have many alternatives

Kendall sellers compete against a large pool of inventory across nearby communities and property types.

Pricing a home correctly requires understanding which properties buyers will realistically consider as substitutes for yours.

A strong strategy should compare recent sales, active listings, condition, upgrades, lot characteristics, HOA costs and location before establishing the asking price.
